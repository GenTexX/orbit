# A review of where Orbit stands - 2026-08-03

Written after the 4.x language-completion phase and before Milestone 5. It covers the whole
project, not only the scripting half: what each part is actually worth, what is broken today,
what M5 needs, and what is worth building next.

## How this was done, and what that is worth

Thirteen independent readers went over the repository in parallel - one per subsystem
(photon+aether, helios, aurora, aurora-wgpu, atlas shell, atlas inspector and theming, comet
frontend, comet backend, and the test/CI/docs apparatus) plus four cross-cutting passes (M5
readiness, an adversarial bug hunt, ADR drift and architecture, and the educational mission).
They produced 124 defect claims, 121 after deduplication. Twenty-five of those were then handed
to adversarial verifiers whose instructions were to *refute* them, and a final critic pass went
looking for what all thirteen had missed.

Two things follow from that, and both matter when reading the rest.

**Verified is a real distinction here.** Findings are marked [reproduced] when someone built a
throwaway crate outside the repo, ran it, and pasted the output; [confirmed] when a second
reader tried to refute it and could not; and [unverified] when it is one reader's claim and
nothing more. Ninety-six of the 121 claims are in that last category. They are not noise (the
ones that were checked held up at an unusually high rate) but they have not been paid for.

That rate deserves a note of its own: **24 of 25 claims survived**, and every one of the thirteen
in the second round was reproduced by running code rather than by reading it. A verifier told to
refute and refuting nothing is a result to be suspicious of. Two things argue it is real here:
the claims were pre-filtered (each came from a reader who cited an exact line), and the second
round's evidence is execution output with isolating controls, not argument. The one claim that
was refuted was refuted on reachability, not on mechanism.

**Nothing was changed while it was written.** The working tree was clean; this is a reading,
not a fix. Every measured number below was taken on this machine today.

**What has been fixed since.** Everything in section 2, and a good deal of sections 4 and 6,
over about thirty commits on 2026-08-03. Section 2 is left exactly as it was written - the
findings are the record of what was wrong, and each one now has a test that fails without its
fix.

All twelve numbered defects are closed, including the three that were still open at the first
pass: the truncating scene write (`write_atomic` moved down into helios, where the scene is
written), `Value::Int` so an exported int reaches the module it was tuned for, and struct
ownership - which needed retain-on-read, release-at-exit and a field access releasing what it
discards to land together, since any one or two alone turns a leak into a double free.

From the rest of the review: a panic hook writes unsaved work to `.orbit/recovered/` (the
"no crash floor" finding in section 4); the frame-depth pre-pass that caused four unlocated
compiler panics in one day is gone, replaced by emitting once and taking the high-water mark;
`last_measure_count` counts the shaping it could not see, and the gutter and placeholder
re-shapes it was hiding are cached; `Style::disabled` reaches input; the `run` example works
again and is tested; a boolean field is a checkbox; broken asset paths and a project that will
not open both say so; an exported variable starts at the value it was written with; and the
Code pane's 28 fields and nine text helpers are their own module.

The 48 claims this document left unverified were then put through the same adversarial pass:
38 stood, 2 were already fixed, 2 were refuted, and 6 went unchecked because two batches hit a
transient error. Those six, and the medium-severity items not yet acted on, are the remaining
work from this review.

## The short answer

Orbit is two excellent pieces of software and one that barely exists, and the gap is now the
whole story.

`comet` and `aurora` are genuinely good. `atlas` is a real editor. `helios` - the engine, the
thing all of it is supposedly for - is about 950 lines of scene model once you take out the undo
system (an authoring feature) and the script host. It has two components, one drawable
primitive, no input, no camera, no lifecycle, and nothing that ticks. The ratio in `ideas.md`
(17.7k lines of editor against 3.5k of engine) understates it rather than overstating it.

Milestone 5 is where that gets found out, and the shape of M5 is not what the roadmap sentence
suggests. The **hard** half is built: comet compiles and runs, `ScriptHost` binds a module to a
live node's transform, `start`/`update`/`on_destroy` all exist and are tested, a trap already
names the comet function it happened in, and `ScriptComponent::reconcile` is ADR 0008's field
migration, already written and tested. The **easy** half does not exist at all: nothing in atlas
has ever constructed a `ScriptHost` - grep the workspace, the only callers are helios's own
tests. There is no Play button, no loop, no play/stop state, no input, no camera, no file
watcher, and `voyager` is six lines with an empty `[dependencies]`.

So the first demo is closer than it looks - the frame loop already computes a clamped `dt` and
already re-reads the scene into sprites every frame with no Ui rebuild, so ticking scripts is
roughly a five-line insertion at `main.rs:6203`. And the milestone is further away than it
looks, because the substrate those five lines land in has no headroom, no concurrency, and no
crash floor. That tension is the most useful thing in this document, and section 4 is about it.

One thing outranks all of it, though, and it is not about M5. **The containers added in
iterations 4.5 and 4.6 - structs, arrays, enum payloads - have systematic ownership bugs.** Six
of them, all reproduced by running code: `copy()` on an array of strings traps inside the
allocator on the fourth frame, `get()` hands back a reference nobody owns, a struct holding a
String leaks it forever, an enum carrying an array leaks it, overwriting an array element leaks
what was there, and an enum carrying a struct emits a module wasmtime refuses to load. The same
measurement that found them also ran an M4-era script for 36,000 frames without growing memory by
a single page - so this is not a weak memory model, it is four hand-maintained tables that have
drifted apart. That is section 2's middle, and it should be fixed before anything is built on it.

## 1. Where each part actually stands

| Part | Lines | State | Trust it under M5? |
|---|---|---|---|
| comet frontend | ~7k | Excellent design, three reachable process-kills | Not without the crashes fixed |
| comet backend | ~8k | M4's parts proven over 36,000 frames; 4.5-4.6 containers are unsafe | No - six reproduced memory defects |
| aurora | 12.6k | Second most mature thing here | Yes, except the Code pane on a long file |
| aurora-wgpu | 2.3k | Small, tidy, correct; no limits anywhere | Yes |
| photon + aether | 1.8k | Honest and well-shaped; does one thing | Yes for mechanism, no for looking like a game |
| helios | 3.5k | Static half mature, dynamic half absent | No - the persistence seam is broken |
| atlas | 17.7k | Mature in its testable parts, thin at the edges | No - no running mode, no snapshot, no host |
| spectrum + prism | 1.4k | Works; prism cannot preview what it edits | Not relevant to M5 |
| voyager | 6 | A stub with an empty dependency list | It is the thing M5 is supposed to build |

Three of those deserve expanding.

**comet is the best-argued code in the repository and the least robust against text a person can
actually type.** The poison-type discipline works - one mistake really does produce one
diagnostic. `check_hook` catching `func update()` with the wrong signature is a piece of teaching
most toy languages never build. And then three separate inputs kill the process outright, all of
them reachable from the Code pane, which runs the whole frontend in-process on the live buffer
on every keystroke.

**aurora's caching discipline is excellent and stops exactly at the draw list.** A 4000-line
non-gutter text area re-lays-out in 0.00 ms with `last_measure_count` at 0. The same area with
the gutter on costs 79 ms per `draw_list()`, because `emit_gutter` is quadratic in document
length - and `last_measure_count()`, the instrument built precisely to catch this class of
regression, reports 0 while it happens.

**atlas is a 7227-line, 109-field, 234-method god object held together by discipline.** The
frame order in `draw()` is documented invariant by invariant and `check_editor_in_sync` is an
actual runtime assertion over the mirror problem - that is a level of rigour most editors never
reach. But "rebuild the whole Ui on any change" is not a choice atlas made; aurora offers no way
to remove a widget, so the whole-Ui swap is the only structure available. Everything downstream
of that - six hand-written widget-to-model sync functions, a `pointer_down` freeze on the
pollers, a per-case focus-restore block - is compensation. The one piece of state that never got
a sync function, `go_to_line`, has exactly the bug the others were written to prevent.

## 2. What is broken now

Ordered by what it costs the user. Everything here is either reproduced or survived an
adversarial verifier.

### Data loss

**1. A script's `@export` values are written to the scene file and silently dropped on load.**
[reproduced, five readers independently]
`crates/helios/src/component.rs:201` - `ScriptComponent::set` only writes a name already present
in `self.exports`, and `serialize.rs:121` builds the component with `Component::from_type_name`,
which is a default with an empty `exports`. So every stored value returns `false` and is
discarded; `reconcile_script_exports` then re-creates each field at its *type* default. The
fields reappear looking correct and reading zero.

This is in the committed demo project right now: `scenes/main.ron` stores `("speed", F32(45.0))`
for `scripts/tunable.cmt`, and every open resets it to 0.0. ADR 0022's central claim - the
inspector owns the value, and it is the value that runs - is not true across a save/load. M5's
hot-reload migration is being built on a persistence path that does not persist.

The root cause is architectural rather than local: ADR 0016 designed `Reflect` when every
component's field set was static, ADR 0022 made one component's field set dynamic, and nobody
added a "declare this field" operation to the contract.

**2. `ctrl+O` reloads the project and clears the undo stack with no confirmation.** [confirmed]
`crates/atlas/src/main.rs:3078`. The guard is only `!code_context_focused()`, so with the scene
tree, the inspector, the viewport or nothing focused, the chord your fingers know as "open"
discards every unsaved scene edit and the entire history. The title's unsaved mark disappears
too, so nothing reports the loss. `CloseRequested` gets a full three-answer confirm dialog; this
gets none.

**3. The scene is saved with a truncating write while scripts are saved atomically.**
`crates/helios/src/project.rs:63` uses `fs::write`. `atlas/src/main.rs:676` has `write_atomic`
and uses it for the Code pane, with a comment explaining exactly why. The larger and less
replaceable artifact got the weaker treatment.

### Crashes reachable from ordinary use

Four confirmed, all of which take the process down with the unsaved scene and the unsaved script
buffer in it.

**4. A backslash followed by any non-ASCII character panics the lexer.** [reproduced]
`crates/comet/src/lexer.rs:224` - the escape handler advances one *byte*, so `pos` lands inside
a UTF-8 sequence and the next slice panics. Every keystroke in the Code pane runs this.

**5. A self-referential struct stack-overflows the checker.** [reproduced]
`crates/comet/src/check.rs:844` - `slots` and `default_value` both recurse through struct fields
with no equivalent of the `building` guard that protects enums. `struct A { b: A }` plus `let x:
A;` aborts the process. The declaration alone produces no diagnostic at all. ADR 0024 states "a
struct cannot contain itself" as an accepted cost; nothing enforces it.

**6. A non-ASCII character before the caret panics the completion anchor.** [reproduced]
`crates/atlas/src/main.rs:950` - `word_before` does `rfind(...).map_or(0, |at| at + 1)` and then
slices there. Paste a line of prose with an em dash or a curly quote into a comment and the
editor dies. The same idiom appears four more times in comet's language service, and - found by
the critic pass, in a file nobody reviewed - a third time in `crates/aurora/src/find.rs:375`,
where ticking "Whole word" on a file containing one accented character panics the framework.

What makes this a defect rather than an oversight is that the careful version is forty lines
above it in the same file: `word_before_or_around` walks `char_indices` and checks
`is_char_boundary` first, and `Ui::byte_to_cursor` carries a comment explaining the hazard.

**7. An ordinary expression on the right of an array-element assignment panics the compiler.**
[reproduced] `crates/comet/src/codegen.rs:1096` - `stmt_depth` takes the max of the value's frame
depth and the place's, but emission *nests* them. `a[0] = a[0] + a[1]` panics with "index out of
bounds", no source location, no diagnostic. So do `a[0] = b[c[0]]` and three other shapes. This
is the first thing anyone does with an array.

### Memory safety in the containers 4.5 and 4.6 added

This is the most serious cluster in the review and it was the last thing found, because it needed
execution to see at all. Six defects, every one reproduced, all in the same seam.

Set against them, the good news, because it is the same measurement: a plausible M5-era script
using Vec2 arithmetic, `str()`, string concatenation, per-frame array churn and `print` was run
for **36,000 frames - ten minutes at 60 fps - and linear memory never left its first 64KB page.**
The parts Milestone 4 exercised are genuinely solid. The parts added in 4.5 and 4.6 - structs,
arrays, enum payloads - are not.

**13. `copy(a)` corrupts the heap when the elements own anything.** [reproduced]
`crates/comet/src/codegen.rs:897` - `emit_array_copy` does a `memory.copy` of the element words
and nothing else, so both arrays end up owning one refcount. `release_array_slots` then walks
both, and every element is released twice. Measured with `Array<String>`: **trap on frame 4**,
inside `comet_alloc`, following a free-list next pointer no allocation ever produced. The
controls isolate it exactly - the same script with `let b = a;` instead of `copy(a)` survives,
with `Array<f32>` survives, with no copy survives.

**14. `get(a, i)` hands back an unowned reference.** [reproduced]
`codegen.rs:2148` copies the element's slots into the `Option` payload with no `retain_value`,
where `Index` at line 2187 does retain. So the `Option` is a second reference nobody took, and
dropping it frees a block the array still points at. Watched directly: the block holding a string
starts at refcount 1, the first `get` drops it to 0, the block goes on the free list, and every
further `get` decrements the freed block's header. ADR 0023 states the opposite rule in so many
words - "reading a local or global that owns something makes the stack copy a second owner".

**15. A struct that owns a String leaks it, permanently, per construction.** [reproduced]
`codegen.rs:1271` - `FnGen::new`'s `owned` filter and `release_owned` have no `Type::Struct` arm,
so a struct local is never walked on the way out. Measured: 200,000 constructions grew linear
memory by 3.99 MB and never gave any of it back; the identical loop with a plain `String` local
stayed flat at one page. The general walker `release_slots` already handles `Type::Struct`
correctly, which is what ADR 0024 says the rule is - so this is an omission at one site, not a
decision.

**16. An enum whose payload is an `Array` is never released.** [reproduced]
`check.rs:452` - `owns_str` counts only `Type::Str` and other enums, so `holds_str` stays false
and every release path in codegen skips the payload. Proven by flipping it: adding a
String-carrying variant to the same enum makes `holds_str` true and the leak disappears. But the
flag cannot simply be flipped, because `retain_enum_slots` (`codegen.rs:1602`) has no `Array` arm
either - with `holds_str` true, binding an array payload in a `match` double-releases and traps.
The two halves have to be fixed together.

**17. Overwriting an array element never releases what was there.** [reproduced]
`codegen.rs:1835` - `Place::Element` computes the address, releases the *handle*, and stores the
new slots over the old ones without reading them. `Place::Local` and `store_global` both release
the previous value first; this path does not. Every `a[i] = v` on an owning element type leaks.

**18. An enum carrying a struct emits a module wasmtime refuses to load.** [reproduced]
`check.rs:1206` instantiates every non-generic enum - computing its payload width via
`Checker::slots` - *before* struct fields are resolved at `check.rs:1214`. So `slots(Struct)`
sums an empty field list and returns 0, and the payload region is sized zero while codegen writes
the struct's real slots into it. `comet::compile` returns `Ok`; wasmparser then says "values
remaining on stack at end of block" and wasmtime refuses to compile `update`. Generic enums are
unaffected, because they instantiate lazily, after the fields exist - so `Option<P>` works and
`enum E { None, At(P) }` does not. The bug is also maskable: if another variant is at least as
wide as the struct, the zero is hidden and the module is correct, which is exactly the kind of
intermittency that costs a day to diagnose.

**What these six have in common** is worth more than any of them individually. The layout and
ownership knowledge is duplicated across four hand-maintained tables - `Checker::slots`,
`owns_str`, `val_types`, `owns_reference` - plus four release/retain mirrors, three of which end
in a wildcard arm that quietly means "owns nothing". Adding a container type to the language is
supposed to fail loudly in every place that has to learn about it; today it fails silently in
most of them. That is the `collect_literals` lesson the roadmap already records as having bitten
three times, in a different corner of the same file.

### Confirmed and smaller

- **The colour picker swallows the click that dismisses it.** [reproduced]
  `main.rs:2937` - `close_color_picker` sets `dirty`, so the press that dismisses the picker
  triggers a full rebuild before the release, and the fresh `Ui` has no armed press. Every
  toolbar button or tree row clicked while a picker is open needs a second click.
- **The scene texture cache is never invalidated.** [reproduced] `textures.rs:50` short-circuits
  on both the loaded map and the failed set, and nothing ever clears either. A texture that
  failed once stays broken for the session, and editing a PNG in another program never shows.
  `Thumbnails` has an `invalidate()` called from five places; this has none.
- **Every image dropped into the viewport gets the demo sprite's size.** [reproduced]
  `main.rs:3402` passes `self.texture_size`, decoded once at startup from the demo project's
  `assets/sprite.png` and never updated. Drop a 512x64 banner and it arrives square.
- **There is no way to create a script, a scene, or any file from inside the editor.**
  [reproduced] `MenuAction`'s only creation action is `NewFolder`. `ctrl+shift+M` attaches an
  empty Script component but writes no file; the browse button lists only files that already
  exist. A learner's only options are to duplicate an existing `.cmt` or leave the tool.
- **The RON round-trip tests cannot see the export bug.** [reproduced] The fixture builds its
  `ScriptComponent` with `..Default::default()`, leaving `exports` empty - precisely the case
  where the broken load path is a no-op. Four tests pass with export loading entirely broken.
  This is the "a test that checks nothing" pattern the roadmap warns about, sitting on the one
  mechanism M5 is built on.

### Traced but not executed

One I found while reading and did not prove, so treat it as a lead rather than a finding:
`delete_selection` (`main.rs:2206`) never clears `self.picker`, and `Scene::node` indexes the
slotmap directly (`scene.rs:77`), so it panics on a stale key. Open a sprite's tint picker, press
Delete, then touch the picker: `set_field_color` runs against a dead `NodeId`. The related claim
that inspector field helpers index `components[index]` unchecked (`ui.rs:199`) was rated latent
because nothing could shrink a component list - the per-component remove button added on
2026-08-03 is what changes that, so it wants a look.

### Silent wrong answers

**8. A `for` body's tail expression runs after the counter is incremented.** [reproduced]
`crates/comet/src/check.rs:1682` appends the `i = i + 1` assignment to `body.stmts`, and codegen
emits statements and only then the tail. `for i in 0..3 { print(str(i)) }` - written the way a
language with tail expressions invites - prints 1, 2, 3. And `for i in 0..len(xs) { xs[i] }`
compiles clean and indexes one past the end.

**9. `func f() -> f32 { if c { 1.0 } else { 2.0 } }` passes the missing-return check and traps.**
[reproduced] `check.rs:3001`. `if` is a statement (deliberately, ADR-level), so codegen drops the
block tail and closes the function with `unreachable`. The natural Rust-shaped thing to write
compiles to a runtime trap with no explanation, when the right diagnostic already exists three
lines away.

**10. Rename rewrites field names and Vec2 axes that merely share the symbol's name.**
[reproduced] `crates/comet/src/service.rs:955`. Renaming a local `x` in a function containing
`transform.position.x = x` produces `transform.position.speed`, which no longer compiles. The
function's own comment states the invariant it violates.

**11. An exported `int` or enum never reaches the running script.** [reproduced]
`crates/helios/src/script.rs:371` picks the wasm type from the `Value` discriminant, and `Value`
has no `Int`, so atlas stores an int export as `Value::F32`; `global.set` then fails against the
module's i32 global and the error is discarded by `let _ =` at line 387. A module with
`@export let n: int;` handed 5 prints 0. `atlas/src/main.rs:733` claims "narrowing back happens
at the wasm boundary"; nothing narrows anywhere.

### Performance

**12. `emit_gutter` is O(n^2) in document length.** [reproduced twice, independently]
`crates/aurora/src/ui.rs:3866` calls `numbers.layout_runs().nth(run.line_i)` inside a loop over
every run, and cosmic-text's `LayoutRunIter` has no `nth` override. Measured, in release,
through the two calls atlas makes unconditionally every frame:

```
  250 lines    0.86 ms/frame
  500 lines    2.19 ms/frame
 1000 lines    6.34 ms/frame
 2000 lines   22.26 ms/frame
 4000 lines   78.85 ms/frame
```

Bounded by the fact that only the *active* pane of a dock leaf is built, so this is paid only
when the Code tab is fronted. Which is M5's headline use case. A 2000-line script open puts the
editor at 45 fps before a single script instruction runs.

## 3. What was claimed and turned out not to be true

Worth recording, because it is what the verification bought.

- **The aurora-wgpu colour-space finding is wrong.** The claim was that the image pipeline
  samples an sRGB texture and a linear one through the same shader, so the viewport and the
  thumbnails cannot both be correct. They can, and they are: photon's scene target is
  `Rgba8Unorm` holding linear light and user images are `Rgba8UnormSrgb`, and the hardware
  sampler does exactly the right thing for each. `photon/src/renderer.rs:466` documents the
  contract. What survives is narrower: no surface configuration ever checks that the driver's
  first reported format is sRGB.
- **photon's severities were rated against a path the editor does not take.** atlas builds
  `Renderer::from_gpu` with no surface; the windowed pipeline and the surface-resize path are
  used only by `sandbox`. The interesting consequence nobody drew: voyager, when it becomes a
  standalone binary in M6, will be the first serious user of photon's windowed path since M1 - a
  path with no pixel test and no daily exercise.
- **The M5 reviewer's "the hard half is built" rests on the one mechanism five other readers
  call broken.** `ScriptComponent::reconcile` does work in memory, and it is ADR 0008's
  migration. But after any restart the values it migrates are always the type defaults, so it
  migrates nothing a user tuned. Both statements are literally true; the optimistic one is
  materially misleading.
- **The infinite-loop hang is real but is not a current defect.** Nothing configures fuel or
  epochs, and `while true {}` in `update` never returns - verified. But nothing calls `update`
  today, and `script.rs:23` documents that driving scripts per frame is M5's job. It is an M5
  prerequisite, not a bug report.
- **helios has no RON parser of its own.** It uses the `ron` crate with serde-derived DTOs, so
  the "a tree deeper than 30 levels cannot be loaded" finding is `ron`'s `recursion_limit`, not
  helios's - which matters because the fix is `ron::Options` on the load path, not helios code.

## 4. The three facts underneath everything else

The single most useful observation in the whole review came from the critic pass, and it is not
a defect. It is that thirteen readers each produced an inventory of *things*, and none of them
described the substrate all of those things stand on.

**Orbit is one process, one thread, one unconditional frame.**

**One process.** The compiler, the language service, wasmtime, the renderer and the user's
unsaved work share an address space by decision (ADR 0002). And there is no panic hook, no
`catch_unwind`, no autosave, no recovery file, no crash log - verified, zero occurrences in
54k lines. This review found eight reachable crashes and one hang. Every reader priced its own
finding as high or medium. Nobody priced the absence of insurance, which is the thing that makes
the price identical for all nine: total loss of the session. The cheapest fix in this document
is a panic hook that dumps the Code pane buffer to `.orbit/recovered/`, and it downgrades every
one of those crashes from catastrophic to annoying.

**One thread.** Zero `thread::spawn` in the workspace; one `async fn`, the adapter request.
Texture decode, thumbnail decode, the recursive directory scan, the language service on every
keystroke, and - for M5 - `comet::compile` on every hot reload all queue behind each other
inside the frame. M5's watcher fires a compile inside a frame. Nobody costed that.

**One unconditional frame.** `ControlFlow::Poll` plus an unconditional `request_redraw`, so a
completely idle editor does a full taffy layout, a full lex of the open script, and a full draw
list every iteration. Measured on this machine: **~3000 fps, 0.33 ms per frame, on a blank
screen** - a core spinning to display nothing. (The window here may not be vsync-throttled; on a
throttled desktop it would sit at the refresh rate and still do all the work instead of
sleeping.) This is in `ideas.md` as "a dirty-driven redraw"; what is new is the number, and that
the same frame is where every M5 addition lands.

Two more absences, both verified by grep, both invisible in any individual review:

- **Nothing in the workspace is DPI-aware.** `scale_factor` appears zero times. winit hands
  physical pixels; aurora lays out and shapes text in them directly. On any 2x display the whole
  editor renders at half size. This is in `ideas.md`, but it silently invalidates every
  first-session judgement in this document, which assumes the window looks right on a stranger's
  machine.
- **atlas has never observed a key release.** `main.rs:1095` guards the keyboard arm on
  `ElementState::Pressed` with no `Released` arm, and `KeyEvent::repeat` is never consulted, so
  OS autorepeat is indistinguishable from a fresh press. A game asks "is left held *now*", which
  is a state question, and the application cannot answer it about any key. Aurora's `InputEvent`
  has no release event at all and its `Key` enum is 22 text-editing verbs, so M5's input cannot
  route through aurora's focus model even in principle - it has to be a second path straight
  from winit. That is a design decision nobody has put on the table.

## 5. What Milestone 5 actually needs

### Already built, and better than expected

`ScriptHost` compiles and caches per path, shares one `Module` across nodes, and instantiates
per node. `start`, `update` and `on_destroy` exist and are tested. A trap names the comet
function it happened in, and `comet::service::function_line` turns that into a line number.
`ScriptComponent::reconcile` migrates exported values across a source edit and is tested. The
frame loop computes a clamped `dt` and re-reads the scene into sprites every frame with no Ui
rebuild. `Scene` is `Clone`, and slotmap keys survive a clone - so a snapshot for Stop is a few
lines that keeps every `NodeId` atlas holds valid. (Do *not* snapshot through RON:
`Scene::from_ron` renumbers every id.)

### Missing entirely

Nothing owns or ticks instances. No `NodeId -> ScriptInstance` map, no update order, no
lifecycle. No play/stop state and no gate on the mutating paths - viewport drags, delete,
paste, inspector commits and the history shortcuts are all unconditional. No scene snapshot. No
input in the host schema: `PROPERTIES` is `transform.position/rotation/scale` and nothing else.
No Camera component, so nothing says what Play renders from. No file watcher of any kind. And
voyager is six lines.

### What will not survive Play, in its current form

1. **A runaway script wedges the editor.** No fuel, no epoch, no store limits, and comet's
   memory is declared `maximum: None`. `while` is comet's only loop and forgetting the increment
   is one keystroke.
2. **Script output forces a full Ui rebuild.** `poll_console` sets `dirty` on any log-ring
   change, throttled to 150 ms, and a rebuild drops aurora's retained press, hover and caret
   state. A script printing each frame rebuilds the shell about seven times a second while the
   user is typing in the Code pane.
3. **The inspector freezes.** `build_editor_ui` reads the scene at rebuild time, so during Play
   the numbers show whatever they were at the last rebuild. The mechanism to fix it already
   exists and is used by viewport drags: `sync_inspector_transform` writes fields in place with
   no rebuild. It covers only the transform today.
4. **The host accessors fail silently.** `get_f32`/`set_f32`/`get_vec2_*` all end in catch-all
   arms (`_ => 0.0`), and `get_bool`/`set_bool` are unconditional stubs. Adding a row to
   `PROPERTIES` without the matching arm compiles cleanly and reads zero - which is exactly the
   failure the roadmap says bit three times during 4.x, and M5 adds several rows at once.

### The decisions to take before writing code

- **What does Play run on?** Recommend cloning the scene into a play scene and restoring on
  Stop, not editing in place.
- **The buffer or the file on disk?** Recommend Play saves first, and the reload path never
  writes into `script_text`. A watcher that touches the editor buffer would be the fourth
  instance of the mirror bug.
- **Fuel or epoch?** Recommend epoch interruption with a per-frame deadline: it costs nothing
  when nothing fires, and "one frame took forever" is a wall-clock question. Either way an
  instance needs a `stopped` state, or one bad frame produces an error every frame forever.
- **Does a hot reload re-run `start`?** Recommend no - a script whose `start` sets the position
  would teleport the node back on every save. Run `start` only on Play, and amend ADR 0008 to
  say so, because it can currently be read either way.
- **Whose values does a reload preserve?** Recommend the component's, consistent with ADR 0022.
  Note that nothing reads globals back out today, despite a codegen comment claiming the host
  can; a script that mutates its own exported variable loses that on reload. Defensible, but it
  should be written down.
- **What shape is input?** `HostType` is F32/Bool/Vec2 with no String, so `key("left")` is not
  expressible without new host functions. Recommend v1 is a fixed set of schema properties
  (`input.left/right/up/down/action`, `input.mouse`) - rows in `PROPERTIES` and arms in the
  accessors, nothing in the compiler. The named action map is a later iteration.
- **Which camera?** A `Camera` component, first in pre-order, falling back to the editor camera
  when the scene has none, so Play demoes before anyone has added one.
- **Where does the loop live?** In voyager, from the first commit. Writing it in `atlas::State`
  and moving it later is the wrong direction, and M6 needs it out anyway. There is exactly one
  place where a scene, a renderer, a texture cache and a window all meet - `atlas::State` - and
  everything M5 needs will fit there easily, and nothing will stop it going there. That is how
  ADR 0002 gets violated by omission rather than by argument.
- **Multiple scripts per node.** Already possible, so instances must be keyed by
  `(NodeId, component index)`, not `NodeId`.

### A proposed order

The M5-readiness pass proposed starting with the decisions. The critic pass disagreed, and I
think the critic is right: the small fixes come first, because they are what makes the rest safe
to run.

**Before M5 starts.**

1. Panic hook writing the Code pane buffer to `.orbit/recovered/`; the export save/load fix;
   `emit_gutter`. All small. The first two are insurance on everything after them, and the third
   is the frame budget M5 spends.
2. The container ownership cluster (section 2). Not because M5 needs arrays - it does not - but
   because M5 is the milestone where scripts run every frame for minutes at a time, and a leak
   or a double free that was invisible in a unit test becomes a trap in the viewport with the
   editor's own memory underneath it. Fix the four mirrors exhaustively rather than one arm at a
   time, and add `comet_heap_used()` first so the fix has an oracle.

**M5 proper.**

3. The decisions above (a day).
4. Epoch budget plus a `stopped` state in helios. Medium, and first among the code because
   everything after it is then safe to run.
5. `voyager::Runtime` owning a `ScriptHost`, the instance map, and `start`/`update`/`destroy`.
   Medium. It can start as fifty lines that only tick transforms.
6. atlas play/stop state, toolbar buttons, scene clone on Play and restore on Stop. Medium.
7. Call `runtime.update(dt)` in the frame loop. Small. **At this point the bounce script
   visibly moves the sprite** - that is the demo.
8. Drain `take_printed_tagged` to the console without setting `dirty`; route a trap to a console
   line plus a squiggle. Small.
9. Refresh the inspector in place via the existing `sync_inspector_transform` mechanism. Small.
10. Input. Medium. Before you would call it Play.

Then hot reload: an mtime poll for `.cmt` files modelled on `poll_settings` (small), the
`apply_exports` type fix (small), and forget/recompile/reconcile/re-instantiate/swap with
keep-old-on-error (medium).

Deferrable past the first demo: fixed timestep, pause and step, the action map, script-driven
spawn and despawn, `on_destroy` on node deletion, the Add Component UI that a Camera component
unblocks, and play-in-a-separate-window.

## 6. Ideas worth having

Only the ones not already in `ideas.md`, grouped by what they serve. Sizes are rough.

### The educational claim

The most actionable single fact in this review: **the curriculum is already written and the index
is missing.** The seven demo scripts are a teaching sequence in the right order - bounce (state,
hooks, vectors), counting (int vs f32), shapes (structs as values), lists (references), states
(enums and match), tunable (`@export`), squiggles (what a diagnostic looks like) - each opening
with prose that explains the concept rather than the syntax. Nothing in the editor knows they
exist.

- **A Lessons pane** listing those seven in teaching order, each opening in the Code pane.
  Small. Highest ratio of educational value to work in the project.
- **New Script from a template, and an Edit button on the Script component.** Today a learner
  cannot create a `.cmt` file from inside the editor at all - `MenuAction`'s only creation action
  is `NewFolder` - and getting from a node to its behaviour means hunting the file in the
  explorer. Small, and it closes the two breaks in the authoring loop.
- **"What your code became": a typed-IR and WASM panel.** This was iteration 4.9 and was cut.
  It should come back framed as the flagship teaching artifact rather than a compiler-debugging
  aid. Nobody else can copy it: Godot cannot show you what GDScript became because it has no
  types to show, and PICO-8 cannot show you Lua's bytecode meaningfully. Medium.
- **Explain-this-error: diagnostic codes with long-form explanations.** `Diagnostic` already
  carries a message and a span. "expected `f32`, found `bool`" is the most common error a
  beginner will ever meet and it currently teaches nothing about why the two differ. Medium.
- **A glossary hover wired to CONTEXT.md.** The definitions are already written and curated;
  hovering "Node" or "Component" in the editor's own chrome could show them. Tiny, and it is the
  purest form of "the tool explains its own concepts at the moment they are needed".
- **Script-facing debug draw** (`draw_line`, `draw_circle`, `draw_text` for one frame). Turns
  invisible quantities visible - draw the velocity vector you just computed. Medium, and it
  needs photon shape primitives, which `ideas.md` already wants for the gizmos.
- **Deterministic time and a seeded random in the host schema.** Two rows plus a builtin. It
  matters for teaching specifically: a lesson that says "you should see the sprite land here"
  only works if the run is reproducible.
- **Self-checking challenge scenes.** A goal expressed as an ordinary Comet script on a hidden
  node, pass or fail reported after a Play run. Needs nothing new once Play exists, and it turns
  the demo project from things to read into things to do.
- **Play-session time travel.** Record every node's transform per frame into a ring buffer and
  scrub backwards after Stop. `Transform` is three fields, so a thousand frames of a hundred
  nodes is a few megabytes. It answers the beginner's most frequent and least answerable
  question, and it is cheap here specifically because the scene is small and value-typed.
- **A stepping debugger for Comet.** Emit an optional debug build where each statement calls a
  host function carrying its span, and pause the loop inside it. Large, but the in-process
  runtime is what makes it possible at all.

### Structural

- **`Ui::adopt(&old)`** - let the framework migrate retained state across a rebuild, keyed by a
  caller-supplied stable id. That deletes about a hundred lines of hand-restore in atlas and
  closes the hazard the roadmap calls a structural lesson. Medium, and it is the single change
  that most reduces future bug volume.
- **Make `WidgetId` carry a Ui generation.** An id from a discarded Ui currently resolves,
  silently, to whatever widget occupies that slot in the new one. A few lines and a
  `debug_assert` turn a class of silent wrong-widget corruption into a panic that names it.
- **Split an `EditorCore` out of `State`** - everything that needs no window and no device. The
  largest testability win available: today `impl State` needs an `ActiveEventLoop`, a window and
  a real GPU, so none of the editor's actual behaviour is reachable from a test, and all 21
  tests in that 7227-line file are on free helper functions.
- **Extract the Code pane from `main.rs`.** About 2050 method-lines and 30 of State's 109 fields
  belong to it, and its couplings outward are narrow. Do it before M5 adds a mode, a snapshot, a
  host and a toolbar to the same object.
- **Add `Value::Int`.** Two layers currently work around its absence and the result is a silent
  no-op on int and enum exports. One variant, an inspector field that refuses a decimal point,
  and an `Int -> Val::I32` arm.
- **Move the reflect-migration walk down into helios.** `default_for` and
  `reconcile_script_exports` live in atlas, which means ADR 0008's hot-reload migration is
  currently implemented in the editor, in a function the runtime cannot call.
- **A stable node path** (`Scene::path(NodeId) -> "root/player/weapon"`) and re-binding by it.
  NodeIds do not survive a load; script instances, selection, ADR 0011's override lists and M6's
  package format all need name-based identity. Small function, four features unblocked.
- **One boundary-safe word-scan helper.** Three implementations of "the identifier around this
  offset" exist; the two unsafe ones crash on the same input. comet already exports
  `is_identifier` - export the scan beside it.
- **An exhaustive match instead of a wildcard in the four release/retain mirrors.**
  `release_slots`, `retain_value`, `retain_enum_slots` and `drop_value` are meant to be four
  views of one predicate and three end in a wildcard that quietly means "owns nothing". This is
  the `collect_literals` lesson, which the roadmap records as having bitten three times.
- **Count frames by emitting, not by walking the tree again.** Delete the parallel
  `block_depth`/`expr_depth`/`place_depth` pass and take a high-water mark from a throwaway
  emission. It makes the entire class of unlocated emission panic impossible rather than fixing
  one instance of it.

### Testing and CI

- **Make the committed demo project the end-to-end fixture.** Load it, save it to a tempdir,
  assert the RON is byte-identical, assert every asset and script path it names exists. Twenty
  lines. It catches the export-loss bug, any future serialization drift, and a dangling
  reference in the one project every user opens first.
- **A round-trip test that actually round-trips a tuned script.** The existing fixture uses
  `..Default::default()`, which leaves `exports` empty - the exact case where the broken load
  path is a no-op. This is the "a test that checks nothing" pattern the roadmap already warns
  about, sitting on the mechanism M5 is built on.
- **A property test over `Scene` RON with generated field values.** A person chose the fixture
  values, which is why the export case was missed. A generator filling `exports` with each
  `Value` variant would have found it on the first run.
- **A counted-work assertion that draw-list size is independent of document length.** The
  specific assertion that would have caught the gutter defect.
- **A test that asserts the dependency graph.** Every layering claim in this review was
  checkable by grep, which means it is checkable in CI - no `wgpu::` outside three crates, no
  `std::fs` in aurora, no `helios` in comet. A dozen lines that pin the property the ADRs care
  most about.
- **`comet_heap_used()`** exported from every module: sum the free list, ten instructions. It
  gives the test suite an exact leak oracle - which is what would have caught the memory defects
  in section 2 - and it gives the editor a live-bytes readout no other engine shows a beginner.
- **Run every ignored test in the lavapipe job**, not just photon's. Three ignored tests have
  never run in CI, one of which is the only end-to-end proof that a node's world transform
  reaches real pixels.
- **cargo-deny with a licence allowlist, before M6 rather than during it.** 489 crates reach the
  build and M6 redistributes all of them. Pair it with a root LICENSE: there is no LICENSE file,
  no `license` field in any of the eleven manifests, and five DejaVu fonts are compiled into
  every binary with their licence referenced only as a URL.

### Renderer

- **Sampler policy as data, `Nearest` by default.** One global Linear/Linear sampler means every
  zoomed pixel-art sprite in the editor is a blur, with no way to change it. Godot's Sprite2D
  makes the same default choice for the same reason.
- **Give `SceneTarget` `COPY_SRC` and make the three vacuous tests real.** The editor's actual
  render path - `render_to_target`, `render_runs_to_target`, `overlay_to_target` - has no pixel
  coverage: the tests that name those functions assert through `pick`, which renders into its
  own private buffer. Emptying `overlay_to_target` leaves all 15 photon tests green.
- **A `RenderStats` return value and a debug pane that draws the glyph atlas itself.** Draw
  calls, instances, batch breaks, glyphs rasterized, atlas occupancy. Orbit is a teaching engine
  and "here is why your batch broke" is the teaching version of a profiler.
- **Make the glyph atlas survivable before it is full.** It cannot grow or evict, and overflow
  is a wgpu validation error under the default handler, which is a panic. Even "clear and
  re-rasterize on overflow, and log it" turns a crash into a hitch.

## 7. What I would stop doing

- **Stop working the code-editor backlog** except the running, debugging and teaching sections
  its own preamble flags. The pane is already better than the engine under it, and competing
  with VS Code is a losing race that does not serve the mission.
- **Stop the aurora extraction programme** until a second application actually needs a piece.
  The stated rule - aurora grows when a second caller proves the need - is right; the extraction
  list is refactoring toward an audience of one.
- **Stop growing prism and spectrum.** 1.4k lines serving theme authoring, a need no learner has.
- **Do not build the node editor / visual scripting graph.** It is large, and it would compete
  with Comet - which is the differentiator. If a beginner does not have to read Comet, Orbit's
  best asset is off the critical path.
- **Resolve the north-star game now.** The roadmap defers it to "revisit at M5", which is now.
  Not for scope control, but because the tutorial game, the thing Play is first pressed on, and
  the first Game Package should be one artifact, and choosing it retroactively costs more.

## 8. Three questions only you can answer

Each one changes the ranking above, and I could not resolve them from the repository.

**Q1. What is your tolerance for losing an unsaved session?** If "not acceptable", the top of
the M5 list is not Play - it is a panic hook, a `.orbit/recovered/` dump and the epoch budget,
roughly two days, after which the eight crashes become annoying rather than catastrophic and can
be fixed on their own schedule. If "acceptable, it is my machine and I save often", fix the eight
individually in severity order - and note that the boundary-safe scan helper is one function that
closes three of them at once. The answer changes again at M6: the moment someone else runs
voyager, "it is my machine" stops applying.

**Q2. Is atlas meant to run on a machine that is not this checkout, and if so, when?** Three
findings collapse into one project if the answer is "before M6": DPI (the editor renders at half
size on any modern laptop), the compile-time-baked `project_dir` with no argv path, and the
missing LICENSE. If the answer is "not until after M6", DPI stays a backlog item and the
first-session analysis in this document should be read as "on Philip's machine".

**Q3. When you say "edit the script while the game runs", do you mean the Code pane is fronted
and being typed in during Play, or that the file changes on disk and the game notices?** These
are two different milestones wearing one sentence. If it is the second - an external editor, or
Play in a second window, with atlas watching mtime - then the gutter cost is a background
annoyance, the rebuild-on-console-output problem mostly evaporates, and M5 is genuinely small.
If it is the first - the demo where you change a number in the code and watch the sprite respond
- then `emit_gutter`, the in-place inspector refresh, the console-without-rebuild path and the
input seam are all prerequisites rather than polish, and M5 is roughly twice the size the
roadmap sentence implies. The roadmap and ADR 0008 are both compatible with either reading, and
every reviewer silently assumed the first.
